# Israeli commercial television rate card: research findings

Researched 2026-08-02. Source for Reshet 13 was used throughout. Where Reshet 13
data was unavailable, Keshet 12 (קשת 12) is noted explicitly. All figures are in
ILS unless stated otherwise. Where a figure could not be confirmed from a real
source it is marked **unknown**.

---

## 1. Audience measurement system

**Operator:** Kantar Media, contracted since 1998 by the Israeli Audience Research
Board (IARB / הוועדה הישראלית למדרוג). Current contract runs through 2030.
Source: [Israel Awards Kantar Seven-Year TV Measurement Contract](https://www.mrweb.com/drno/news29039.htm)

**Panel:** 700 households, approximately 2,200 individuals, equipped with Kantar
Focal Meter devices. The Focal Meter captures both traditional broadcast viewing
and streamed or internet-accessed content on all devices in the home. Data is
delivered through Kantar's Instar analytics tool.
Source: [IARB Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%94%D7%95%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%93%D7%94_%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A3%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA_%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92)

**What one rating point means:** One TVR (Television Rating) point equals one
percent of the total Israeli Jewish TV-household universe. The IARB measures the
general population and segments by age, gender, religion, income, and geography.
Source: [Targetet TV ratings guide](https://targetet.co.il/%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%99%D7%98%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%92-%D7%94%D7%98%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%95%D7%99%D7%96%D7%99%D7%94-%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9A-%D7%9C%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%A9%D7%99-%D7%93%D7%99%D7%92%D7%99%D7%98%D7%9C/%D7%9E%D7%90%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D/)

**GRP vs TRP:**
- GRP (נקודות רייטינג ברוטו): total rating against the whole population,
  accumulates across all airings of a campaign.
- TRP (נקודות רייטינג קהל יעד): rating against a defined target demographic,
  for example women 25-54. Media plans typically specify a TRP goal; the channel
  guarantees delivery or provides make-good spots.

**Settlement:** Payment is determined by actual rating points achieved, not a
fixed number of spots. The channel's traffic system books spots against the
advertiser's CPP rate and reconciles at the end of each week.
Source: [Media Pulse](https://www.mediapulse.co.il/he/%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%98%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%95%D7%99%D7%96%D7%99%D7%94/)

**Delayed viewing:** The IARB counts live viewing plus time-shifted viewing up to
2:00 AM the same night. Catch-up viewing on the following days is reported
separately and is generally not included in the traded currency.

## 2. Pricing model: CPP (cost per rating point)

Israeli commercial television is sold on a **CPP (cost per rating point)** basis.
The advertiser negotiates a CPP for a 30-second spot in a given daypart and target
audience. Revenue = CPP x actual rating points delivered x (spot length / 30).

This is identical in structure to the existing engine's formula:
`revenue = base_price_per_second_per_tvr_point x spot_seconds x tvr_points x premiums`.
A 30-second spot at 1 TVR on the base engine yields `60 x 30 x 1 = ILS 1,800`.
At the CPP figures below, a 1-TVR prime-time spot on Reshet 13 would be around
ILS 1,500 for 30 seconds, implying a per-second-per-TVR base of ILS 50. The
existing base of ILS 60 sits in the correct order of magnitude but is above the
mid-market Reshet 13 level and closer to Keshet 12. See section 7 for the mapping.

**No public rate card exists.** Both Reshet 13 and Keshet 12 negotiate rates
privately with agencies and advertisers. Prices vary by season, channel demand,
advertiser volume, and presence of a digital package. All figures below are from
third-party reporting or industry sources, not from published rate cards.

## 3. Daypart definitions

The following daypart structure is used consistently across Israeli industry sources.
Time boundaries follow Israeli broadcast convention (Sunday-Saturday, with prime
defined as 20:00-23:00 under the Second Authority law).
Source: [Media Pulse](https://www.mediapulse.co.il/he/%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%98%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%95%D7%99%D7%96%D7%99%D7%94/)

| Daypart Hebrew name | Time range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| בוקר (morning) | 07:00-09:00 | Lowest rates |
| בוקר מאוחר (late morning) | 09:00-11:00 | Budget tier |
| צהריים (daytime) | 11:00-15:00 | Mid tier |
| אחר הצהריים (afternoon) | 15:00-19:00 | Rising |
| סמי-פריים (semi-prime) | 19:00-20:00 | Higher |
| חדשות (news) | 20:00-21:15 | Premium; exact boundary varies by channel |
| פריים / סופר-פריים (prime) | 21:15-23:00 | Highest rates |
| לילה (late night) | 23:00-01:45 | Lower than prime |

Note on "news" as its own daypart: Reshet 13 and Keshet 12 both treat the main
evening news as a premium inventory slot distinct from the entertainment prime that
follows. The Globes article confirms Reshet 13 prices the news block separately
(see section 4).

price increase round, which included the CPP schedule before and after the hike.
These are the only CPP numbers found from a named Israeli source; they are not
from a published official rate card.
Source: [Globes: Reshet raises advertising prices up to 15%](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001268924)

| Daypart | CPP (ILS per rating point, 30-second base) |
|---|---|
| סמי-פריים | 800-900 |
| פריים רגיל (standard prime 20:00-23:00) | ~1,500 |
| פריים פרמיום (shows like The Voice, Survivor) | 1,850 |
| האח הגדול (Big Brother) | 1,950 |

|---|---|---|
| Keshet 12 (קשת 12) | 45,000-120,000 | 12,000-30,000 |
| Reshet 13 (רשת 13) | 22,000-65,000 | 8,000-20,000 |

## 6. Spot lengths and ad format types

**Standard commercial spots (תשדירי פרסומת):**
- Sold in: 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, and up to 90 seconds (90s is the regulatory maximum
  per Second Authority rules; 30s is the market standard base).
- Pricing relative to 30s: 15s is priced at approximately 60-75% of 30s rate.
  5-10s commercial spots are non-standard and unusual in the spot market; see
  sponsorship below.
- The 30-second spot is the universal trading unit. All CPP figures above refer
  to 30-second spots.
Sources: [Natan Elimelech media agency](https://natanelimelech.co.il/%D7%A2%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%98%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%95%D7%99%D7%96%D7%99%D7%94/);
[Second Authority regulations 1992](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/4941.htm)

**Sponsorship notices (הודעות חסות):**
- Maximum duration per notice: 10 seconds (regulatory). In practice the market
  standard is 6 seconds for most programs and 6 seconds for sports.
- Placement: immediately before the program starts, immediately after it ends,
  or adjacent to commercial breaks within the program. Must not run during news.
- On-screen text "הודעת חסות" must appear throughout.
- Required spoken phrase: "התכנית מוגשת בחסות..." (program presented with
  sponsorship of...).
- Maximum sponsors per program: not directly capped in the regulations except
  for sports (7 per half with possible extension to 10).
- Pricing for sponsorship: ILS 3,000-8,000 per airing at low-rating dayparts;
  ILS 10,000-12,000 per airing at prime time.
Sources: [Second Authority sponsorship rules 2009](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/73408.htm);
[Exioma media agency](https://exioma.co.il/%D7%A2%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%98%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%95%D7%99%D7%96%D7%99%D7%94/);
[Moked: Channel 13 advertising](https://www.moked.co.il/tv_channel_13.php)

**Billboard / billboard-style formats:** Not a distinct named format in Israeli
TV. The closest is the sponsorship notice (above) which can include a visual
billboard frame. The term "billboard" is not used in Israeli industry vocabulary;
the Hebrew equivalent is "הודעת חסות" or "תשדיר חסות".

## 7. Position premiums within a break

Israeli channels sell position in break as a premium. The following figures are
from the Globes article on Reshet 13's price structure:
Source: [Globes](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001268924)

| Position | Premium |
|---|---|
| First (נעיצה ראשונה) | +20% |
| Second | +15% |
| Third | +10% |
| Last (נעיצה אחרונה) | +10% |

These premiums are consistent with the existing engine's config (which has first
position at +30%, second at +15%, third at +5%, last at +20%), though the exact
values differ. The market confirms the direction: first and last positions command
a premium, middle positions are baseline.

## 8. How a campaign is committed

Israeli TV campaigns are structured in one of two ways:

**GRP/TRP package (standard for larger advertisers):**
The advertiser and agency agree on a TRP target for a defined audience (for
example 150 TRP against women 25-54) within a date range, at a negotiated CPP.
The channel guarantees delivery; if actual rating points fall short, make-good
spots are added at no charge. The total budget is CPP x TRP target.
This is the standard approach for national brand campaigns.

**Flat-spot buying (used by smaller advertisers):**
The advertiser buys named spots in named programs at fixed prices. No GRP
guarantee. Payment is for airtime, not for delivered audience. Smaller campaigns
(ILS 30,000-80,000/month) often use this approach via media agencies or
channel resellers.

**Seasonality:** Prices vary up to 35% between low and high-demand periods.
High-demand periods in Israel are typically September-December (post-summer
return, holidays, year-end) and January-March (post-holiday spend). Summer
(July-August) is low season.
Source: [Media Pulse](https://www.mediapulse.co.il/he/%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%98%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%95%D7%99%D7%96%D7%99%D7%94/)

## 9. Regulatory constraints on advertising volume

Source: [Second Authority regulations on TV ad placement, 1992](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/4941.htm)

- Maximum ad time: 10 minutes per hour (general rule).
- Prime time window (20:00-24:00): maximum 40 minutes total across the block,
  which works out to 10 minutes per hour on average over 4 hours.
- Sponsorship time cap (20:00-24:00): maximum 3 minutes 36 seconds of
  sponsorship notices per 4-hour prime window.
- Maximum single spot length: 90 seconds.
- Minimum transition between program and break: 3 seconds.
- Breaks must fall at natural program pauses ("אתנחתה").

The existing engine config has `max_regular_breaks_per_hour: 4` and
`max_gold_breaks_per_hour: 2`. The Second Authority regulations do not cap the
number of breaks directly; they cap total minutes. Four breaks averaging 2-3
minutes each would be 8-12 minutes per hour, above the 10-minute regulatory cap.
This may be an inconsistency in the config to review: four 2-minute breaks
already hit the limit, leaving no room for gold breaks if they add duration.

## 10. What a "package" means in Israeli TV

Unlike US "upfront packages", Israeli TV does not publish named packages with set
names and GRP levels. A "package" in Israeli industry vocabulary means one of:

1. A negotiated annual or quarterly commitment at a volume-discounted CPP,
   sometimes called a "חבילה שנתית" (annual package).
2. A combined TV plus digital deal, where buying TV time from a channel unlocks
   preferential access to the channel's digital video inventory.
3. A named program sponsorship arrangement: the advertiser sponsors a specific
   show (for example the weather segment, a reality show) for a season, buying
   the 6-second sponsorship notices across all episodes.

No published package structures, GRP tiers, or volume thresholds were found from
any official source.

## 11. Key target audiences used in buying

The IARB measures multiple demographic cuts. The most commonly referenced in
Israeli agency material:

- כלל האוכלוסייה / כלל הצופים (all viewers): the broadest GRP base.
- 18 פלוס: adults 18 and over, the standard commercial audience.
- נשים 25-54 (women 25-54): the most widely cited TRP target for FMCG and
  retail campaigns.
- גברים 25-54 (men 25-54): used for automotive, finance, and sports content.
- ילדים 4-14 (children 4-14): used for children's programming, toy campaigns.

The existing engine uses "TVR" as its rating unit without specifying the
demographic base. In market practice, a quoted "TVR" on Reshet 13 refers to the
all-viewers base (כלל הצופים) unless a target demo is specified. A campaign brief
would typically state: "150 TRP, נשים 25-54, פריים".

## 12. Mapping the real rate card to the existing engine

The existing engine in `kairos/optimize/pricing.py` uses:
`base_price_per_second_per_tvr_point = 60.0` ILS.

**Structural match (good):**
- The market sells on CPP (ILS per rating point per 30-second spot).
- The engine computes `base x seconds x tvr x premiums`, which is equivalent:
  at 60 ILS/s/TVR a 30-second 1-TVR spot = ILS 1,800.
- The market's Reshet 13 prime CPP is ~1,500 ILS for 30s at 1 TVR, implying
  a per-second-per-TVR base of ~50 ILS.
- The engine base of 60 ILS is above Reshet 13 mid-market and closer to Keshet
  12 levels. It is within the plausible range.

**Layer match (partial):**
- `program_type_premiums`: correct in concept. The market does use content-type
  premiums (news, prime show, other). The exact multiplier values are not
  publicly confirmed.
- `day_of_week_premiums`: Friday and Saturday premiums (existing config: 1.15
  and 1.20) are consistent with the Israeli market's weekend premium. Israeli
  week is Sunday-Saturday; Friday and Saturday are the high-demand days.
- `position_in_break`: the market confirms first and last positions command a
  premium. The engine's existing values (1.30 first, 1.20 last) are plausible
  but not confirmed from a published source. The Globes article suggests
  +20% first, +10% last, somewhat lower than the engine's defaults.
- `ad_type_premiums`: the market distinguishes sponsorship (חסות), commercial
  spot (פרסומת), and channel promo (פרומו). The engine has these. Promo at 0.00
  (zero revenue) is correct: channel promos do not generate advertiser revenue.
  Sponsorship at 1.05 is within the plausible range.

**Gaps between engine and market:**
1. The engine has no concept of a TRP target demographic. It uses TVR (total
   viewers) only. In market practice, campaigns are committed against a specific
   demographic TRP (e.g. women 25-54). This affects how a campaign's GRP target
   is expressed in the engine.
2. The engine has no concept of a campaign GRP commitment or make-good. It
   schedules breaks and computes revenue but does not model the TRP-guarantee
   contract between channel and advertiser.
3. Spot length trading: the engine prices by the second, which is equivalent to
   the market's CPP-x-(length/30) model. The market treats 30s as the atomic
   unit with 15s at ~60-75% of 30s rate (not 50%), so there is a slight pricing
   asymmetry for short spots. The existing engine charges linearly by the second;
   a short-spot discount is not modeled.
4. Seasonality: the market charges up to 35% more in high-demand periods. The
   existing engine has day-of-week premiums but no calendar-seasonal layer
   (though the events layer can serve this purpose when activated).
5. The regulatory 10-minute-per-hour ad cap is not enforced in the engine as a
   hard constraint. The config has a break-count cap (4 regular + 2 gold), not a
   minute cap. A 4-break, 2-minute-each schedule is 8 minutes and stays under
   10 minutes, but a 4-break, 3-minute schedule hits the limit.

**What does not need to change:**
The CPP pricing model structure (base x seconds x TVR x premiums) is correct for
the Israeli market. The engine's arithmetic is right. The input values
(base = 60, specific premium multipliers) are configurable and can be tuned
to match a specific channel's actual negotiated rates. Nothing in the research
invalidates the structural approach.
