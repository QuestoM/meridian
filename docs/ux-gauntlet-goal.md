ultracode

Rebuild the Meridian operator experience until it beats the bar. The full brief
is docs/ux-gauntlet-prompt.md: read it first and follow it. This is the
standing condition.

Re-specify what Meridian is for, discover who lives in it and what each of them
does when they sit down in the morning, then rebuild the architecture, the
flows, the screens, the backend and the AI integration until a person doing
that job would rather use Meridian than the best tool they have ever used.

Not a user interface project. Build whatever the jobs require: endpoints,
stores, entities, integrations, permissions, performance, model work. Nothing
is a dead end: every name, number and status opens what it refers to.

Hold the line between training and runs. Training is ours, the startup: fitting
coefficients, held-out gates, model rebuilds, drift and data coverage, company
staff only, on its own surfaces with its own dashboards. Runs are theirs: the
weekly plan, recompute, the daily schedule, forecasts, constant and routine.
Nobody may ever wonder which of the two a button does.

The bar, judged by critics against real artifacts, never summaries:
1. Stopwatch. Job stories measured in a browser on the running app: seconds,
   clicks, keystrokes, dead ends, guesses. Ten seeds are in the brief; complete
   and freeze the set during discovery.
2. Blind A/B against named references: Linear for speed and keyboard control,
   Stripe for money legibility and honest empty states, Google Ads for campaign
   hierarchy and pacing, a professional editing timeline for the break pod,
   Frame.io for per-asset technical status, Figma for direct manipulation, the
   best in-product AI you can find.
3. Three-way. Today's Meridian against the new one against the reference. If
   today wins anything, that is a gap that goes back to the builder.
4. Laws. Honest math and tri-state honesty, the competitor boundary, engine
   numbers frozen unless a change is a proven fix, canonical Hebrew vocabulary,
   the Israeli week, RTL correctness, no em-dashes or emojis or exclamation
   marks, files under 450 lines, the full suite green at 1438 passing.

How to run it. Discovery before any edit: the people, their job stories, the
measured current state, what is missing entirely, and the architecture the jobs
imply rather than the one the engine's internals imply. You decide the
decomposition into the smallest pieces that can be improved and judged
independently. Every piece gets a builder and a separate blind critic with
fresh context that inspects the real rendered artifact. A builder never grades
itself. The critic names the single largest remaining gap and sends only that
back. There is no round limit; do not do three rounds and stop. A piece ends
when the blind comparison stops picking the reference and stops picking today's
Meridian, or after three rounds that fail to close the named gap, which you
record and escalate. Finish with one fresh integration critic over the whole
product, then fix what it finds.

The model is in scope and must be the best this data can support: keep the
retention model, the audience model and the optimizer measured and gated,
improve them where measurement supports it, and never assert a coefficient.
Where the data cannot decide, say so and say what data would.

Boundaries. No deploys, purchases, messages, credentials or irreversible
actions without explicit approval. Never delete a capability
before proving it dead or replaced. Never fabricate a number to fill a screen.
Commit in logical commits and push when a piece is coherent and green.

Workbench. Keep docs/ux-gauntlet/workbench.html live: after every round record
what changed, the evidence, the critic's verdict, the largest remaining gap,
the next action, and anything deferred with its reason. Never a claim you did
not measure.

This is a floor, not a ceiling. It is not everything that is needed and not
everything the owner knows he needs. Find the rest, build it, and keep going.
