# Meridian / Kairos product model

Kairos is the operator-facing product built on Meridian's modeling foundation: a Hebrew-first, RTL operations system for Israeli commercial television. It turns programme supply, daily traffic orders, audience estimates, commercial terms, pricing, and regulatory rules into break plans intended to maximize projected advertising revenue while protecting viewer retention and on-air constraints. Its promise is not generic analytics. It is a control surface for deciding what airs, when it airs, what it earns, what could go wrong, and how to recover.

The people using it span thirteen job profiles but three permission roles. General managers and viewers need a fast answer about money, health, and decisions. Planners and schedulers build the week and adjust the day. Traffic operators assemble and verify the spots inside each break. Yield, account, and campaign managers own booked value, delivery pace, and make-goods. Compliance, data, account, and model stewards govern rules, sources, users, and model releases. Job should shape the first useful surface; role and affiliation should govern authority.

Frequency and stakes determine the redesign priority. Daily work carries the product: morning triage, traffic-file ingest, pacing exceptions, tonight's pod assembly, and same-day schedule corrections. Weekly planning is the next-highest-frequency loop: set an objective, run, compare, freeze, and publish a reversible plan. Onboarding, rate-card changes, user administration, compliance edits, and model activation matter greatly but occur episodically. The live sample plan represents roughly ₪40.9M in modeled gross advertising revenue for the owned channel, while decisions also carry retention, regulatory, delivery-liability, and live-air technical risk. Modeled money is not billed money, and the interface must preserve that distinction.

The three product-carrying paths are:

1. **Plan the week:** state the commercial objective, run optimization, compare alternatives using revenue net of retention cost, and freeze a named, reversible version.
2. **Control the broadcast day:** inspect the real timeline, move or resize breaks with the consequence visible before save, populate each pod from traffic, verify media and duration, reorder, and lock.
3. **Manage commercial delivery:** turn an order into agency, advertiser, campaign, flight, and terms records; surface the worst pacing exceptions first; then accept risk or create and settle a make-good with an auditable trail.

The current product contains the capability, but its information architecture hides the hierarchy. Four rail entries mount one six-section weekly planner. Three commercial entries mount one clients workspace. Reports and Data are two entrances into Sources. Calendar and Pricing are redirects into Rules. Assistant is a dock disguised as a route, and Model is a second overlay application. The result is a large number of labels and destinations without a stable mental model. The overhaul will organize the product around the three core paths, retain governance and recovery as explicit secondary workspaces, and keep assistant/model tools contextual rather than competing with primary navigation.

Important maturity caveat: the intended operating model is clearer than the current evidence. Much commercial data is seeded or demo-marked, delivery truth is incomplete, ratings are planned rather than measured, and there is no authoritative As Run billing source. The redesign must make evidence provenance, freshness, scope, and modeled-versus-observed status obvious instead of visually smoothing over those gaps.
