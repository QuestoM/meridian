## 2. The endpoints

| Method and path | What it answers |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/pacing` | Every campaign on the operator's channel, worst pacing first, with both goal lines and either a verdict or the named reason there is not one. It carries a day count per row and not the days themselves |
| `GET /api/pacing/{campaign_id}/days` | The broadcast days behind one row, read when a reader opens them. Measured, the days were 181,274 of a 365,884 byte board; on their own read the board is 184,610 and a drill is 2,838 |
| `POST /api/pacing/{campaign_id}/accept` | Record that the risk on this campaign stands. Body is `{note}` and carries no figure. Refused on a row the board is not asking a decision about |
| `GET /api/make-goods` | The ledger, newest first, as one timeline in `decisions` plus the two kind-specific lists, with the sign-off rule published as not configured |
| `POST /api/make-goods` | Raise one against a campaign's measured shortfall. Body is `{campaign_id, note}` and carries no figure |
| `POST /api/make-goods/{make_good_id}/state` | Move one. Body is `{state, offer_value, offer_window_start, offer_window_end, note}` |

`GET /api/make-good-alerts` is unchanged, is not wrapped by the wall, and still
answers `data_available: false` with the `campaign_flights.csv` reason. It is the
signal `kairos/optimize/pacing.py` feeds the optimizer with, and two shared tests
assert its exact answer.

All five new routes carry `PACING_WALL = Wall(company_only=False)`: any account
reads, a write role changes, and `can_edit` is stamped on every read.

## 2a. The two endings, because the job has two

JS-6 is done when every at-risk campaign has an act taken against it **or an
explicit decision to accept the risk, and both are recorded**. A ledger of raises
alone leaves a campaign somebody read and accepted looking exactly like one
nobody opened, so the store holds two kinds.

- A **make_good** is raised against a measured shortfall and is offered, settled,
  declined or withdrawn.
- An **acceptance** is the recorded decision that the risk stands. It stamps the
  same measured figures a raise would have taken, derives nothing, never becomes
  an offer, and moves only to withdrawn.

The duplicate check is **per kind**. A shared one would have silently forbidden
raising a make-good against a campaign whose risk somebody took on last week,
which is the ordinary case.

Both kinds ride one ledger read, because a person answering for a campaign has to
see both. `decisions` is the joined timeline and is what a surface renders;
`make_goods` and `acceptances` are the kind-specific lists beside it.

## 3. The honest math, stated so a critic can check it rather than trust it

Two stores are read and neither is written: P4's campaign store, and P4's derived
delivery ledger, which states per campaign per broadcast day whether the traffic
log holds those spots (`aired`, `scheduled`) or holds nothing for that day
(`unknown`).

**The clock is the ledger's, never the reader's.** `counted_as_of` is the instant
the ledger split aired from scheduled at. Nothing in this piece calls the wall
clock, so every figure is reproducible.

**Pace to date is stated only when the elapsed window is complete.** The flight's
broadcast days on or before the counted day are the elapsed window. When every one
of them carries a source, delivered to date is a total and a ratio against it is
real. When one is missing, the verdict is `unknown` with code `gap_in_elapsed` and
the missing dates are listed, because a ratio computed over a hole would tell a
reader a campaign is behind when what is true is that nobody counted a day of it.

**The reference is an even share and says so.** `expected_through_counted_day` is
`goal * days_counted / days_in_flight`, published with its rule in both languages.

**The two triggers are policy and are published.** 0.95 and 0.85, on the payload,
with the sentence that they are the product's stated rule and not a commercial
term, and that the commercial trigger is owner-pending.

**The forward half fails separately.** `covered`, `short_certain` (every remaining
day sourced and still short, so the shortfall is measured rather than projected)
and `not_booked_yet` (remaining days carry no source, so the rest cannot be
projected). Nothing is extrapolated in any of the three.

**The deficit ladder, most certain first.** `measured_closed` for a flight that has
ended with every day sourced, `booked_short` for a running flight whose remaining
days are all sourced and short, `to_date` for the gap against the reference at the
counted day. A campaign that reaches none of them cannot carry a make-good and the
raise is refused with the reason.

**The surface offers a make-good only when the shortfall is owed.** A campaign
behind on day one of seven with unbooked days ahead offers the booking, not a
debt. `tests/test_p11_surface_remedy.py` holds the same behind-pace campaign in
both shapes and asserts the offered act changes.

## 4. What this piece deliberately does not do

- **It does not propose compensating inventory.** The spec's make-good wants the
  offer drawn from the break board. An offer here is the value and the window a
  person entered, and the payload says in both languages that it reserves nothing.
  Drawing an offer from real inventory needs the break's spot-level contents,
  which is P10.
- **It does not decide who signs one off.** The commercial rule was never supplied,
  so the ledger records who acted and publishes `sign_off.configured: false` with
  the path to supply the rule.
- **It does not touch `campaign_flights.csv`.** Owner decision 4's three inputs are
  still outstanding. What became buildable is the delivery ledger P4 derived from
  the traffic file on disk, which is real for the days it covers and honestly
  unknown for the days it does not.

## 5. Open, for the lead: the mount

`tv-break-dashboard/src/clients/pacing/**` is P11's under section 8.2 and it sits

for it, so it is raised rather than reached for.

## 6. The surface did not compile, and nothing in the run could have told you

Measured this round, and the most important thing on this card.

`PacingWorkspace.jsx` opened a `<Figure>` and closed it with `</span>`. The whole
of `src/clients/pacing/**` failed to compile, so applying the three-line mount in
section 5 would have broken the Clients destination on the first attempt, and the
mount would have been blamed for it.

Nothing could see it. `npm run build` transforms 3,510 modules and not one of them
is in this tree, because nothing imports it, so the repo build passed clean with a
syntax error sitting in a shipped file. Every guard on this surface read a
component as text, and text guards pass over a mismatched tag without noticing.
The tests were green, the build was green, and the artifact did not exist.

The fix is one word. The guard is the part worth keeping:
`tests/test_p11_surface_javascript.py::test_every_component_on_this_surface_actually_compiles`
runs `verify-parses.mjs`, which is the real bundler with everything outside this
tree external and the stylesheets stubbed. Proven to fail against the defect and
pass against the fix.

**The general rule, for the lead.** A tree that nothing imports is not covered by
the build, whatever the build says. Every piece in this run that ships a
destination subtree behind an unapplied mount is in the same position, and a
green build is not evidence about it. P13 under P10 is the other one.

Measured after the fix, on a scratch copy carrying the section 5 mount: the tree
compiles, and the bundle grows from 1,623.16 kB to 1,659.43 kB of JavaScript and
from 305.75 kB to 319.41 kB of CSS, which is this surface arriving.
