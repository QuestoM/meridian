# P8 contract: History

The section 8.8 publication for P8: the endpoints it owns, the payload shapes it
emits, the files from section 8.2 it wrote, and the helper modules it created.
Published on delivery of round 1.

The destination answers one question with three halves: what changed, who
changed it, and how to put it back. Before this round the product answered it in
three places that never met, and the fourth record, `output/run_log.jsonl`, had
held every run's provenance since the engine was built with no endpoint reading
it and no screen showing it.

Its Bar 3 row is the restore path itself: the same nine logical files, snapshot
first, and the viewer write-lock survives. Both are pinned by tests named below.

## 1. Endpoints owned

Eight paths, nine operations, four of them writes.

| Path | Methods | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| `/api/history` | GET | the merged timeline, newest first, with the counts, the actors, the sources and today's attestation |
| `/api/history/runs/{run_id}` | GET | one run: what it read, what was in force, what it produced, and the delta against the previous run of the same scope |
| `/api/history/since` | GET | what changed since a calendar day, and the guardrail evidence when nothing did |
| `/api/versions` | GET | the restore points, each with whether it can be put back |
| `/api/versions/{version_id}/diff` | GET | what restoring one would change, per logical file |
| `/api/versions/{version_id}/restore` | POST | put the selected files back, after recording a safety point |
| `/api/versions/snapshot` | POST | a named restore point taken by hand |
| `/api/versions/{version_id}` | PATCH | rename a restore point |

The five `/api/versions` paths moved here verbatim in the wave-zero router split
and are unchanged in shape. The three `/api/history` paths are new.

**Measured OpenAPI diff, on the assembled tree at delivery: 145 paths, 182
operations, 87 writes. No path removed and none redefined.** The three paths
that appeared while this round ran belong to P1 and P6.

## 2. Payload shapes frozen

### 2.1 A timeline entry

Every entry, of every kind, carries the same seven fields, so a surface renders
one row shape rather than six.

```
{id, kind, ts, actor, via, artifact_root, facts}
```

- `kind` is one of six: `change`, `preview`, `run`, `restore_point`, `restore`,
  `sign_in`.
- `artifact_root` is one of three: `data`, `output`, `models`. It is the
  training test of section 4.1 as a data field.
- `via` is `dashboard`, `assistant` or `engine`.
- `facts` is the kind's own payload and carries no prose in either language.
  Every word a person reads is chosen by the surface from
  `src/history/history-labels.js`, which is what keeps the payload past the
  section 4.2 lexicon test by construction rather than by review.

`GET /api/history` also emits `counts`, `total`, `matched`, `kinds`, `actors`,
`scope`, `sources`, `run_scope`, `today`, `attestation`, `note`, `can_edit`.

### 2.1.1 How far the page reaches, added in round two

Two query parameters and six payload keys, all additive. No key was removed, no
key changed meaning, and every existing caller reads the same body it read
before.

`until` is an ISO calendar day in the broadcast zone and keeps entries recorded
on or before it, which is the direction `since` cannot go. `before` is an opaque
cursor taken from `next_before`, and it is the sort key `(ts, id)` the list is
already ordered on, so a repeated step always advances even when one day holds
more entries than a page.

The payload adds `served`, `newer`, `older`, `next_before`, `page_max` and
`window`. `matched` is the whole result set for the day window, the kind and the
actor, and does not move as the reader pages, so `newer + served + older ==
matched` on every page. `counts` and the new `window_total` are taken inside the
day window and before the kind and the actor, so a filter control prints what
clicking it would reveal rather than a figure over a set the reader is not
looking at. That is the one behaviour change in this section: with no day window
set, both are identical to what round one emitted.

### 2.1.2 How far back the record reaches, added in round six

One top-level key and two keys inside each of the three `sources`, all additive.
No key was removed and none changed meaning.

`record_starts` is the oldest broadcast day the merged record still holds, taken
as a minimum over the three sources and over every assembled entry, so it can
never claim a later start than something the page is already showing. Each
source carries `starts`, its own oldest surviving day, and `retention`,
`{pruned, keeps, prune_at, unit}`. A source the product may not serve reports
`starts: null`, because a run log it may not attribute cannot date itself. The
same `record_starts` rides the attestation body of section 2.3, where it sits
beside the guardrail store's own `record_starts` and the two are told apart by
name on the surface.

`starts` is a property of the store, not of the reader: the request recorder's
start is read from the file's first line rather than from the caller's scoped
slice, because how far the evidence goes does not change with who is looking.

### 2.1.3 Whose figure each figure is, added in round seven

Two keys inside `sources.changes`, both additive. No key was removed and one
changed meaning, which is recorded here because it is the defect of section 5.4:
`records` is now the store's own line count rather than the caller's slice of it,
which is the same rule `starts` beside it already followed.

`in_scope` is what this caller may read of that store, and `scope` is the rule
that produced it, `all` or `self`, the same word the top-level `scope` carries.
Only the request recorder is scoped per account, so only it carries the pair:
restore points are the shared operating record, and the runs figure is the
operator's own channel by the competitor boundary, which `run_scope` states in
words rather than as a count of what was excluded.

One action code was added to the closed vocabulary of section 2.1:
`restore_point_renamed`, for `PATCH /api/versions/{id}`. Measured after it
landed: 42 distinct codes in the map plus `other`, and 43 label pairs on the
surface, so every code a route can produce has a word in both languages.

### 2.1.4 Who may put which file back, added in round eleven

Two additive keys, and no key removed or changed in meaning. `GET /api/versions`
and `GET /api/versions/{id}/diff` both carry `file_permissions`, one entry per
logical file: `{can_edit, company_only, guards}` plus `can_edit_reason` when the
answer is false, which is the same string the 403 would carry. `guards` is the
field names inside that file which carry a wall of their own, never a sentence,
so the surface says what they are in the reader's own language.

Each entry in the listing gains `withheld_files`, the covered files this reader
is on the wrong side of the line for. It is a property of the reader, where
`restorable` and `restore_block` beside it are properties of the version.

The listing answers with the file's own wall, which is the strictest thing that
can be said without reading a version. The diff answers exactly, because it
already knows which fields that one version would move.

### 2.2 The runs source is tri-state

`sources.runs.state` is `available`, `unreadable` or
`withheld_no_operator_channel`. The third exists because of a measured breach,
recorded in section 5.

### 2.3 The attestation

`GET /api/history/since` and the `attestation` key of the timeline read emit the
same body: `{day, counts, matched, changed, attempted, refused, outcomes,
attested_kinds, record_starts, examined, scope, guardrails, verdict}`. `changed`
counts only `change`, `restore` and `restore_point`, because a preview saved
nothing and a run read the saved state rather than editing it. `guardrails.state`
is `unchanged`, `changed` or `unknown`, and an unreadable store answers unknown
with its reason, never unchanged.

### 2.3.1 What was attempted and what happened, added in round ten

Three keys, all additive, and one changed meaning which is the defect of section
5.7: `changed` no longer counts a write the server refused. `attempted` is what
`changed` used to be, `refused` is the named figure beside it, and `outcomes` is
`{applied, refused, unknown}` over the `change` kind. `verdict` gains a third
value, `unknown`, for a window whose only entries are acts with no recorded
result, because unchanged is a claim and that record cannot make it.

The same `outcomes` block rides `GET /api/history` beside `counts`, taken over
the same day window and before the kind and the actor, so the figure on the
Change filter and the figure that says how many of them were refused are over
one set. Every timeline entry of kind `change` or `preview` carries
`facts.outcome`, which is `applied`, `refused` or `unknown` derived from the
`facts.status` already beside it, and `GET /api/activity-log` carries the same
`outcome` on every line. The two lines the derivation draws are 400, above which
the server declined before it wrote, and 500, above which it failed rather than
declined and the product will claim neither answer.

### 2.4 A run detail

`{run_id, created_at, channel, day, engine_version, segment_count, summary,
guardrails, assumptions, inputs, comparison, run_scope, run_log_available,
run_log_state}`. `comparison.state` is `measured` or `no_earlier_run`, and each
field inside it is `measured` or `unavailable`. A field only one of the two runs
recorded yields no delta rather than a zero.

## 5. The measured breach this round closed

**`GET /api/history?kind=run` served three rival channels.** Measured on the
running instance at 12:03 on 2026-08-01: 217 rows for `קשת 12`, 124 for
`עכשיו 14` and 76 for `כאן 11`, with their names and their recorded revenue on
them, against 83 of the operator's own.

The cause was not in `channel_scope.py`, which behaved exactly as its own
contract says: with no configured channel it passes every record through and
says so in its note. What had happened is that another surface had written
`operator_channel: ""` into the shared `data/kairos_settings.json` while
testing, and this surface served the result.

**A product that cannot tell which runs are the operator's own may not guess and
may not serve all four.** `_run_records` now withholds every run in that state,
the timeline says so where the counts are printed, and it offers the control
that fixes it. `GET /api/history/runs/{id}` answers 409 with the same reason
rather than serving one. Pinned by
`test_with_no_operator_channel_every_run_is_withheld_rather_than_served`, which
asserts that not one of the four channel names appears anywhere in the payload.

### 5.4 The fifth route it took, closed in round seven: the reader's slice under the store's name

**The footer printed the reader's own slice as the size of the store, and then
contradicted itself in the next sentence.** Measured by a blind critic at 23:22
and 23:41, and reproduced here on 2026-08-01 against a copy of this repository's
own recorder holding 5,261 lines: signed in as an operator the footer read "The
request recorder holds 1 line, which become changes, previews and sign-ins"
directly beside "the request recorder keeps the newest 5,000 lines". As a viewer,
4. As the admin, 5,261. A store that keeps 5,000 and holds 1 is a
self-contradiction printed in one paragraph, and the figure that was wrong was
the one whose whole job is to say how large the evidence is.

2. `history_api_reach.sources()` emits that count as `records` and the caller's
