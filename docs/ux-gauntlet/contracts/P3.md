# P3, Plan: the day and the break

The section 8.8 publication: the endpoints this piece owns, the payload shapes it
emits, the files from section 8.2 it writes, and the helper modules it declares.
Then the evidence for each of the four bars, measured rather than described.

The destination serves JS-3, the scheduler who moves one break, and the break
inspector. Its reference bar is a drawing tool for direct manipulation, selection
and undo, and a professional editing timeline for exact durations.

## 1. What this piece is

The product had no object below the programme segment. The saved plan carries a
break COUNT per segment, and the board a person looked at spread those counts
evenly across the programme as a display heuristic, so there was nothing to
click, nothing to address and nothing to attach money to. Section 3.4 of the
specification makes the break the money layer, and nothing in that section is
true until the break exists.

This piece builds it: a stable break identity, the day it lives on, the money the
plan credits to it, the acts a scheduler performs on it, and the exact inverse of
each act.

## 3. Payload shapes

**Break identity.** `<segment_id>~<ordinal>`, for example
`2024-11-01|רשת 13|008~1`. The separator is a tilde rather than a hash because a
hash in a URL is a fragment delimiter: it never reaches a server unencoded, and
one caller that forgets to encode it turns a break id into a segment id silently.
A tilde is unreserved in RFC 3986.

**Money.** Every break carries `projected_revenue`, which is the optimizer's own
marginal revenue credited to that break at insertion, and `delivered`, which is a
tri-state and never an amount. The break's own record also carries the rating
that credit was computed at, `rating_at_this_break`, and the retention behind it,
because a basis that cannot reproduce its own figure is a wrong basis: see
section 8.
Measured on `רשת 13 / 2024-11-01` at the committed
settings: the 80 break credits sum to **1,067,845.57** against the day's own
**1,067,845.55**, a drift of 0.02 ILS from display rounding against a stated bound
of 0.005 per break.

**The footer prints the sum of the rows it is drawn under, and never the day's own
total.** An earlier round printed the day there, which was true only while nothing
was filtered. With the gold filter on it put 1,028,206 under a column of three
breaks worth 30,488, which is 33.7 times the figure it claimed to total, and with
no gold break in the day it left zero rows, no empty state and the same figure.
Both are closed. The day's own total is now a second line under its own label,
present only while a filter is on, and an emptied filter replaces the table and its
foot with a state that names the mark that is missing. Re-measured in a browser at
1440 x 823 after the fix, marking `001~1` gold from the day board so its three
breaks carry the mark: three rows at 10,712, 10,163 and 9,614, the footer 30,488
against the served credits 10,711.71 + 10,162.61 + 9,613.52 = 30,487.84, the day
line 1,028,206 against the served 1,028,205.58, and with the filter off 79 rows
totalling 1,028,206, which is the day. With no gold break the table and its foot are
gone and no figure is left standing under a row-sum label.

**That day total is the same number a different piece measured independently.**
`W0-5.md` section 5 reports `_optimize_one_day` on this channel-day at
1,067,845.5490407092 and its own scoring seam at 1,067,845.549040709. The board
serves 1,067,845.55. Three measurements, two pieces, one figure, which is the
strongest available evidence that this surface reports the plan's own money and
not a parallel calculation of it.

**Every figure carries its scope.** `basis` on both the board and the score
payload names channel, day, segment count, revenue weight, risk lambda, objective
mode, source and currency, and the surface prints channel and day beside each
figure rather than in a tooltip.

**Three honest states, never a figure.** No day to open (no channel or no plan),
delivered money (no spot ledger covers any planned day), and break contents (the
individual ads are not modelled until the owner's break identifier lands). Each
carries a machine `state`, a reason and a path forward, in both languages.

**A saved placement the plan no longer binds.** `GET /api/plan/day` also carries
`unbound_placements`: the day's saved records whose break id the plan no longer
has, each with `break_id`, `constraint_id`, the position it saved, a machine
`state` of `segment_replanned` or `segment_absent`, a tri-state `restriction` of
`in_force`, `absent` or `unknown`, and a reason and a path forward in both
languages. **The partition is exact and it is the point:** a saved record is
either reachable on its chip or served here, one of the two, never both and never
neither. Section 6 has the measurement that forced it.

**What saving would do, before the click.** `POST /api/plan/day/save-effect`
carries `before`, `after` and `delta` on the day's own totals, `rearranged`,
`compliance`, the `restrictions` it applied with their frozen-contract predicates,
`measured: true` and `engine_ms`. It builds the constraint rows a save would write,
runs the plan's own optimizer with them in force, writes nothing, and reports the
engine's own figures. Verified equal to the written save on both measured cases:
`-30,575.55` and `-47,444.20`, with the break count 80 and 78, gap 0.0.

### 6.1 The clipped number, and the two framings that were missing

**The defect, reproduced.** A critic measured the row above and found it false at
the scale the board opens at. Reproduced in Chrome on `רשת 13 / 2024-11-01` at
1440 x 823: the board opens at 6 px per minute, labelled 1.6x, every break in the
day is 120 s, so all 80 chips are **12 px wide**, of which 4 px is content box
once the 3 px padding either side and the 1 px border are taken. The clock
`00:28:24` wants **47 px** and the length `120s` wants **25 px**, and both were
rendered into 4 px regardless, so every chip on the track printed the character
`0` over the character `1`. **160 rendered labels, 160 of them clipped.** The
numbers only became readable at the maximum scale, 30 px per minute, where the
chip is 60 px and the day track is **46,800 px, 40.7 times the 1,150 px box that
holds it**, with no one-press way back.

**What the chip does now.** `chipLabels` in `day-board-model.js` is given the
width the chip is actually drawn at, taken from the same `positionStyle` that
places it, and the chip renders only a number it can render whole. The thresholds
are the text's own measured width plus the 8 px the padding and border take:
measured at the shipped font, 10 px Inter with tabular figures, no character of a
clock or a length needs more than 6.25 px. So a 12 px chip prints nothing, a
33 px chip prints its length, and a 58 px chip prints both. Measured after the
change, same day and same viewport: **0 numbers rendered inside a chip and 0
clipped**, and at the maximum scale both are printed inside again, which is the
ladder working at both ends rather than a label switched off.

**Where the numbers went.** Beside the object, which is where a drawing tool puts
the dimensions of a rectangle too small to hold them. The badge is drawn above
the chip and centred on it, on hover and on selection, and it is the live readout
of the gesture. Measured with one break selected and the pointer on its
neighbour: **two badges on an 80 chip board, 92 px wide against 12 px chips,
centred to the pixel, both inside the track, neither clipped**, the clock wanting
51 px in a 51 px box and the length 27 in 27. Driven through a real pointer drag,
the badge read `01:13:55` while the typed start field read `01:13:55`. The two
facts were already in the chip's accessible name and stay there, so the badge is
`aria-hidden` and takes no pointer events: a badge that swallowed a press would

minute draws it 2,964 px wide, so a fit that could not go below the floor could
