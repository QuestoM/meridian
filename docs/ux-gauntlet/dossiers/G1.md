## Job stories and their done conditions

**The thesis, quoted from `media-domain-from-the-trade.md`, which outranks the
code:**

> The stated direction is that the channel takes over placement: the agency sends
> a GRP or target-audience goal instead of a spot list, and the channel is
> accountable for delivering it under all its constraints. [...] **This is the
> product's real thesis**, and the goal-based order is therefore not a secondary
> mode. It is the destination.

**G1-a. A trader books a goal rather than a spot list.**
Done when an order can be created carrying a target audience and a points
target, with no spot list at all, and the product accepts it as a complete order.

**G1-b. The optimizer places against that goal.**
Done when a plan run reads the goal and the placement it produces is measurably
different from the same run with the goal absent. Measured, not asserted: the
same channel-day, two runs, the delta named.

**G1-c. The channel can say whether it will deliver.**
Done when, before the flight starts, the product states expected delivery against
the goal with its basis, and states honestly when it cannot.

**G1-d. Delivery is settled against the goal.**
Done when the pacing board reads the goal as the denominator rather than a spot
count, and a shortfall against a GOAL raises the same make-good ledger a shortfall
against a spot list does today.

| `data/campaign_flights.csv` rows | **0. Header only.** |
| Times the OPTIMIZER reads any goal field | **0** |

**The last row is the whole piece.** The commercial layer already models the
goal: it is stored, it is served, it is on screen. It reaches the pacing board,
the delivery ledger and the commitment check. It has never once reached the
engine that places breaks, which optimises revenue net of retention and does not
know a goal exists.

So this is not "add a goal". The goal is there. This is: **carry it across the
seam into the optimizer, and settle against it.**

`campaign_flights.csv` being header-only is owner decision 4 in
`decisions-for-owner.md`. It blocks G1-c and G1-d being measured on real data,
and blocks neither from being built.

## The API surface this piece owns

To be published in the contract before the first edit. It will at minimum extend
the campaigns write path with a goal-only order, and add a read that answers
"what will this goal deliver, and on what basis".

It does NOT own `kairos/optimize/**`. The optimizer takes the goal through a
named seam, the way `pricing_from_settings` carries operator pricing in, and the
seam is a contract both sides publish.

## Reference product, and what to compare

The trade's own description above is the reference, because no product in this
market does it yet. Compare against what an agency does TODAY over email: a block
booking with no client named, then the actual buy about two days out.

Specifically: the order form must accept a goal with **no spot list**, and must
not silently invent one.

## Trade facts that bind this piece

Quoted, not referenced.

> **Agencies over-order prime deliberately.** They ask for more prime minutes
> than they need because they know they will not get them, so the remainder lands
> where they actually wanted it. An order quantity is a negotiating position, not
> a demand forecast.

That one binds hardest. A goal-based order removes the negotiating position, so
the number a trader types means what it says for the first time, and the product
must not treat it as an upper bound to be discounted.

> **The campaign is a living object updated daily**, at best. Mid-day re-planning
> is normal. A design that assumes a campaign is entered once and then executed
> is modelling a business that does not exist.

> Large campaigns arrive **one to two days ahead, not as a whole flight**.

> Cancellation rules are demand-dependent: a day before broadcast billing has
> started and cancellation is refused, but in high-demand periods a channel may
> welcome a cancellation because it can resell.

And the standing law: the Israeli week runs Sunday to Saturday and the weekend is
Friday and Saturday. Data is ISO-keyed; presentation is Sunday first.

## What is already built

- The goal fields, stored and served, on 51 to 55 campaigns.
- The pacing board, which already reads a goal as a denominator and carries four
  separate refusals to state a pace it cannot stand behind.
- The make-good ledger, which settles a shortfall today.
- The commitment check in `campaigns_commitment.py`.
- The audience model: expected TVR with eight gated families, of which
  weekday-slot, series and competitor are ON and the calendar families are
  honestly OFF pending two years of data.

The audience model matters more here than anywhere else in the product, because
a target-audience goal is a claim about audience and this is the only thing that
predicts it.
