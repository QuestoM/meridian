## Step 2: attacks (attack_results.json)

### A1 channel confounding: the headline magnitude is NOT channel-robust

Channel enters the pooled fit as full fixed effects (one dummy per channel,
reference = modal channel), so the control is not weak in form. The problem is
support: length support barely overlaps across channels. Kan 11 (n = 857,
median 2.07 min) has 319 breaks in the 2-minute window but only 14 in the
4-minute window; Keshet 12 (median 4.35) and Reshet 13 (median 5.17) carry the
long breaks. The 2-minute in-support window is 73 percent Kan 11; the 4-minute
window is 85 percent Keshet 12 plus Reshet 13. The pooled linear delta equals
the intercept evaluated at the reference channel, which is Kan 11, a channel
with essentially no 4-minute breaks.

Within-channel refits (same spec minus channel dummies, cluster bootstrap
B = 400):

| channel   | n   | linear delta (frac pos) | quadratic delta (frac pos) | bins delta (frac pos) |
|-----------|-----|-------------------------|----------------------------|------------------------|
| Kan 11    | 857 | +0.109 (0.99)           | +0.210 (1.00)              | +0.038 (0.72)          |
| Now 14    | 261 | +0.232 (0.95)           | +0.240 (0.98)              | +0.205 (0.93)          |
| Keshet 12 | 737 | -0.007 (0.36)           | -0.013 (0.22)              | +0.048 (0.70)          |
| Reshet 13 | 677 | -0.021 (0.31)           | -0.087 (0.02)              | -0.003 (0.39)          |

The two channels with real 4-minute support (Keshet 12, Reshet 13) show NO
positive fixed interruption cost (linear intercepts -0.007 and -0.021) and
their parametric deltas are zero to negative; Reshet 13's quadratic delta is
significantly NEGATIVE (-0.087, 97.75 percent of bootstrap mass below zero).
The large positive deltas live in the short-break channels where s(4 min) is
extrapolation. However, the sign does not consistently flip: nonparametric
in-support deltas within each channel (A2) are +0.008, -0.003, +0.040, +0.036
(frac positive 0.54, 0.46, 0.68, 0.75), i.e. near zero with wide CIs, and
the Reshet quadratic flip contradicts Reshet's own positive raw delta, so it
is model artifact, not a demonstrated negative. Verdict on A1: the pooled
a = 0.0503 (p = 0.038) and the 97 percent confidence are channel-composition
driven and do not survive within-channel scrutiny; the within-channel evidence
is uninformative-to-weakly-positive, not sign-flipped.

### A2 in-support nonparametric delta: direction survives, pooled

No parametric shape, local means only, cluster bootstrap B = 2000:
raw delta 2*mean(shed | len in [1.75, 2.25]) - mean(shed | len in [3.5, 4.5])
= +0.0475 (CI [-0.026, +0.123], 90.3 percent positive); covariate-adjusted
(residualized on the full control set including channel FE) +0.0455 (89.5
percent positive); Gaussian-kernel version (bw 0.5 min) +0.0385 (95.3 percent
positive). The 2-vs-4 delta is NOT an extrapolation artifact at the pooled
level. The caveat from A1 stands: the pooled in-support windows have almost
disjoint channel composition, so "pooled in-support" still leans on
cross-channel comparability after FE adjustment.

### A3 split-scenario independence: contradicted in the measured data

The scenario prices two 2-minute breaks as two independent draws of the
per-break effect. Measured spacing says otherwise: later breaks (ordinal > 1)
whose previous break ended within 15 minutes shed 0.0102 on average vs 0.0459
when the gap exceeds 15 minutes (Welch t = -2.26, p = 0.024, n = 401 vs 488);
breaks with gap-to-previous of 5 minutes or less average -0.015 (a small
gain). Spearman rho(gap_prev, shed) = +0.038 (p = 0.057). Closely spaced
breaks shed LESS per break, so summing two independent 2-minute effects
OVERSTATES the split penalty by a real, sign-known (though confounded and
unquantified) amount. VERDICT.md flags this as unmeasured; it is in fact
measurable and points against the magnitude.

### A4 pipeline leaks: no bug found; clip selection is real but does not
### manufacture the pooled result

- Shed sign convention verified from raw ratios: max abs error 4.6e-16.
- The duplicate-floor-minute join drop (148 keyed rows) touches 0 measured
  rows.
- Clip survival is strongly length-dependent (Spearman rho = 0.361,
  p ~ 3.5e-177): only 10.4 percent of sub-45-second keyed breaks survive vs
  71 to 79 percent at 2 and 8-13 minutes. The measured sample over-represents
  isolated breaks, and by A3 isolated breaks shed more, so the sample the
  curve is fit on systematically resembles the consolidated world more than
  the split world. Direction: overstates the split penalty for the actual
  decision (a split schedule has closely spaced breaks the pipeline would
  drop).
- Refit on uncontaminated breaks only (gap > 3 min both sides, n = 2,259):
  linear a = 0.0641 (p = 0.020), delta +0.0641 (99.25 percent positive); bins
  delta +0.0457 (87.5 percent positive). The pooled result is not created by
  clipped windows.

### A5 selection: reverse-selection story finds no support

Between-title sd of title-mean shed (titles with >= 5 measured breaks,
n = 130) is 0.0903. Erasing a +0.03 to +0.05 delta requires split-favored
titles to be more fragile by 0.33 to 0.55 title-sd, a moderate and not
implausible magnitude a priori. But the direct test rejects it: a within-TITLE
equal-total-minutes split estimator (same title, same rounded total ad
minutes, different break counts; 26 cells, 121 instances) gives +0.0514 per
extra break (CI [-0.030, +0.141], 88.6 percent positive), larger than the
class-cell estimate (+0.019) and same-signed. Holding the title fixed makes
the split penalty bigger, not smaller, which is the opposite of what the
fragile-shows-get-split story predicts.

## Verdict: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED

Confirmed:
- Every headline number reproduces exactly from the real data.
- No pipeline bug: sign convention, join, and cluster keys are correct; the
  result survives restriction to uncontaminated full-window breaks.
- Rejection of superlinearity (regime b) is solid: no specification, pooled or
  within-channel, in-support or parametric, shows split-favoring convexity;
  len_sq is negative pooled (p = 0.046) and within Reshet 13 (p = 0.0007).
- The consolidate-over-split SIGN survives the in-support nonparametric test
  (+0.038 to +0.047, 90 to 95 percent positive) and the within-title
  quasi-experiment (+0.051, 89 percent positive).

Refuted or overstated:
- The fixed interruption cost a = 0.0503 (p = 0.038) is not a stable
  structural constant: within the only two channels that actually broadcast
  4-minute breaks it is -0.007 and -0.021; the pooled value is identified by
  cross-channel composition under a common-slope assumption over nearly
  disjoint length supports, and the linear delta is evaluated at a reference
  channel (Kan 11) with n = 14 breaks near 4 minutes.
- The 97 percent positive-mass headline overstates confidence. The cleanest
  designs give 89 to 95 percent (pooled in-support, within-title) and 46 to
  75 percent within channels, and the independence assumption behind the
  two-break scenario is contradicted by measured spacing data in the
  direction of a SMALLER true penalty.
- Bottom line for the optimizer recommendation: directionally supported
  (weakly), but the 0.03 to 0.05 log-point per-interruption term should be
  read as an upper bound from one month of heavily channel-confounded data,
  not a measured constant; the honest point range from the robust designs is
  roughly +0.02 to +0.05 with 10 to 30 percent probability of sign error,
  before the A3 spacing correction, which pushes it lower.
